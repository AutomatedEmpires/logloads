# LogLoads — marketplace realignment blueprint

Generated 2026-07-24 by a 7-agent research + audit pass (payment rails, pricing economics, four codebase audits, synthesis).
Operating model: host-stated flat driver pay; platform fee charged ON TOP to the host on completed loads only; strictly non-custodial; no free host tier.

> ## ⚠️ Read this before citing anything below
>
> **This document is research input, not the current plan.** It was produced *before* the founder decided the rate, and four things in it are now superseded:
>
> 1. **The rate is 5%, not 3%.** Every "3%", "3.00%", and the `$500 → $485` deduction example below predates the decision. At the decided 5% that example is **$500 → $475**. The authoritative rate lives in `AGENTS.md` section 7.
> 2. **The implementation sequence here is incomplete.** The synthesis agent exceeded its output limit and its narrative begins partway through, at Step 3. Do not follow this sequence — the complete, adversarially-reviewed spec is **`docs/marketplace-implementation-spec.md`**.
> 3. **Phase 0 payment records must never store payout credentials.** Where this document mentions "ACH details", Zelle handles, or check-by-mail addresses, the stored contract is **method + status + optional non-sensitive instructions only** — never account or routing numbers, credentials, or TINs. A record-only MVP must not quietly become a store of financial PII.
> 4. **Uniqueness cannot be enforced in SQL.** Where this document recommends a database constraint against duplicate fee events: the live runtime is a single JSON snapshot row and the SQL mirror is not the execution path. Idempotency and at-most-one-per-slot must be enforced in the zod/service/CAS mutation path; SQL constraints are defence in depth only.
>
> **Authority order:** `AGENTS.md` section 7 (decisions) → `docs/marketplace-implementation-spec.md` (design) → this document (evidence).
>
> What remains valuable here and is *not* superseded: the payment-rail research, the pricing economics reasoning, and the four codebase audits — complete, and the reason the decisions came out as they did.

## 9. RISKS + OPEN DECISIONS

### Risks, ranked by how expensive they are to discover late

1. **The fee base is self-reported.** LogLoads never touches the money, so `driverPayCents` is host-authored and the entire revenue model rests on it. A host posts $400 and tops up $100 off-platform, cutting the fee 20% invisibly. Mitigate in product, not pricing: driver-side confirmation of the amount actually received is part of billable completion (Step 8); posted-vs-confirmed divergence is flagged (Step 9); repeat divergence is a policy violation. The friction favors us — a host who understates must persuade the driver to accept a lower posted number on trust, which is exactly the trust the marketplace exists to remove.
2. **Terminology collision ships a lie by accident.** "Confirmed by the host" already means delivery. If the payment feature lands before the split, drivers will read delivery acceptance as payment confirmation. Step 8 must not merge without the rename.
3. **Custody regression by copy-paste.** Destination charges and separate charges & transfers are the *default* in Stripe tutorials and AI scaffolding. A future contributor will produce a custodial integration that passes tests. This is why it goes in `AGENTS.md` at Step 0 and not in a design doc.
4. **A single nonzero `application_fee_amount` in Phase 1** silently makes LogLoads a company that deducts its cut from driver pay, with no symptom except the driver receiving $485 instead of $500.
5. **Money in a JSON snapshot has no database backstop.** No CHECK, no FK, no unique index. Deterministic fee-event ids plus at-most-one-per-slot assertions are the entire double-billing defense. Every one needs a test that fails if the guard is removed.
6. **Auto-confirm is a billing lever pointed at the host.** 96h auto-accept both fixes the park-just-short-of-billable hole and creates a "you billed me for a load I never accepted" support conversation. It must be disclosed at host onboarding, in the terms, and on the delivery card *before* the window expires.
7. **Host cancels after the haul but before confirmation** yields no fee and no platform remedy for the driver's run. The incident record is the driver's only evidence. Expect this to be the first ugly support case.
8. **Collections is the flip side of non-custodial.** LogLoads bills 3% after the work is done, for value already delivered, with only a card on file. Host non-payment is a first-class failure mode, not an edge case — dunning plus publish suspension is required at Step 7, not later.
9. **Driver adverse selection.** A marketplace that explicitly does not guarantee payment competes against platforms offering quick-pay. Host payment-reliability history is the substitute — but only because it is driver-attested, which is why the host may never set "received."
10. **Adding `abandoned` will pass typecheck with a hole.** `Record<>` maps get flagged; `!==` comparisons do not. Manual grep plus a driven-UI check in that PR.
11. **1099 responsibility moves the moment a rail exists**, and the `controller.fees.payer` choice that protects driver pay is the same choice that hands LogLoads the filing duty.
12. **Merchant-of-record surprise (Phase 1):** the host's statement shows the *driver's* business name, not LogLoads. Disclose at host onboarding and set the statement descriptor deliberately.
13. **KYC gate (Phase 1):** an unverified driver cannot be paid through the rail. Accept-eligibility must depend on payout readiness or the Phase-0 fallback must be visible.
14. **Governance drift.** If Step 0 does not land first, a future agent reads `AGENTS.md:112` and reverts the revenue model as prohibited work.

### Decisions that genuinely need the founder

1. **Confirm 3.00% flat + $49/mo minimum (credited, waived in month one) + Dispatch Pro explicitly additive.** This is a price, not an architecture choice. Everything downstream is built around it.
2. **The existing "free during the launch pilot" public promise.** It is live copy today. Moving current hosts to paid needs a dated effective date, advance notice, and no retroactive charging. Recommendation: 60 days notice, no grandfathering, no free tier — but this is a public promise being changed and it is the founder's to change.
3. **Auto-confirm: 96 hours, and does auto-confirmation bill?** Recommendation: yes, it bills — otherwise silence is a free ride. This is the single most contestable billing rule in the design.
4. **Minimum and maximum `driverPayCents`**, and whether per-slot pay may vary within one series. Recommendation: allow variation (a 2 AM slot is worth more), default all slots to the series number.
5. **Automated consequences for at-fault abandonment.** Recommendation: counters from day one, enforcement switch default OFF, revisit with 90 days of data.
6. **Deadhead / dry-run compensation.** A driver who runs and cannot deliver has no record type in this design. Recommendation: not in v1 — naming the gap beats inventing a half-feature. Founder decides whether v1.1 adds a zero-fee `goodwill_payment` record.
7. **Whether LogLoads ever charges a cancellation fee.** Current design: no, in either direction.
8. **Phase 1 go/no-go and the `controller.fees.payer` choice** (drives the 1099 duty). Requires counsel first.
9. **W-9 collection in Phase 0** — counsel; likely over-collection.
10. **Hours-of-service.** `softDailyDutyMin: 840` warns and does not block. Whether LogLoads ever *blocks* on HOS is a legal determination, not an engineering one.

### One thing worth saying plainly

The founder's model is more buildable than it looks, because three of its four pillars already exist and work: the parent-series capacity ledger is real and enforced, the billable-completion state machine (`pending → submitted → confirmed → disputed`) is real and tested, and the non-custodial posture is already correct in both code and legal copy. What is missing is narrower than the audits' volume suggests — **a stated pay number, an addressable slot, a fee event, a payment record, and a conflict guard.** The single largest genuine hole is scheduling: a driver can double-book today through the main path, and that is a correctness defect independent of any of the money work.
